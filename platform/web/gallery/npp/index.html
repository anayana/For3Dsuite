Nitrogen deposition, production and belowground allocation in temperate and boreal forests

An exploratory look at the forest sites of the global net primary production database by Rodal, Luyssaert, Balzarolo & Campioli (2025, Scientific Data, CC BY 4.0): how total production and the share going belowground relate to nitrogen deposition, climate, soil and management.

Which variables matter most?

Random forest (500 trees) per response. Bars: drop in model fit when one variable is shuffled (permutation importance, mean ± sd of 50 repeats). The cross-validated R² (5-fold, 10 repeats) shows how well the model predicts sites it has not seen. Where it is close to zero or negative, the ranking should not be read as a result.

Data and method

  • Sites: biome “forest”, climate region “cold” or “temperate”. NPP values in g dry mass m⁻² yr⁻¹, averaged per site over all measurements.
  • Belowground share: BNPP / total NPP (NPP total 1). Wood share: aboveground wood NPP / total NPP.
  • Nitrogen deposition: total wet and dry deposition from the database (GEOS-Chem based, 2.0° × 2.5°), year nearest to the measurement, converted to kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹.
  • Climate and soil: WorldClim bioclimatic variables (mean annual temperature, annual precipitation, aridity index) and the dominant HWSD v2.0 topsoil unit (pH, C:N, sand) as stored in the database.
  • Analysis: Spearman rank correlation with nitrogen deposition, random forest with permutation importance (Python, scikit-learn). Code: analyse.py.

Limitations

  • Only sites with both NPP and BNPP enter the share analysis (about 60 sites). That is small for ten predictors.
  • The deposition grid is coarse. Site-level deposition, deposition history and local fertility are not captured.
  • NPP methods differ between studies, especially for fine and coarse roots.
  • Climate and deposition are correlated across sites, so the correlations shown here are not effects.

Data: Rodal, M., Luyssaert, S., Balzarolo, M. & Campioli, M. (2025). A global database of net primary production of terrestrial ecosystems. Scientific Data 12, 1534. doi:10.1038/s41597-025-05773-4 · dataset doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.29085488 (CC BY 4.0). Basemap: Natural Earth via world-atlas.
