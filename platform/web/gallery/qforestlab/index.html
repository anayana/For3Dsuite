Canopy structure and light from open terrestrial laser scans — four forests, one method

Open TLS data from Q-ForestLab (Ghent University, CC BY 4.0): tropical savanna, temperate deciduous forest, alpine conifer forest and tropical rainforest, every tree labelled by hand, plus a large old tropical tree scanned from the ground and from the canopy. All five are interactive 3D scenes; below, the same structural analysis for all plots — vertical profiles, strata and light at the forest floor — and the next step towards microclimate.

Key figures

PAI proxy = projected plant area (leaves + wood) per ground area, from voxel cover; comparable between the plots, not an absolute PAI. Light = share of light reaching 1 m above ground: vertically, and as a daily mean along the sun path (weighted by sun elevation) at the plot's latitude.

Vertical profile

Tropical rainforest: dense through the whole height, crowns up to 36 m. Savanna: sparse layer between 5 and 15 m. Ofental: very dense crowns between 5 and 15 m (steep alpine conifer stand). The upper canopy is underestimated wherever the ground scanner sees little — strongest in the rainforest.

Light reaching the ground

Vertical transmission through the voxel column. Daily means along the sun path are in the table above; in the rainforest and at Ofental almost no direct sun reaches the floor.

Strata

PAI proxy per stratum. Strata relative to canopy height H (P95): ground < 0.5 m, understorey 0.5–2 m, lower 2 m–H/3, middle H/3–2H/3, canopy > 2H/3.

Next step: microclimate

Light at the forest floor is only one part of microclimate buffering. The process-based model ForEdgeClim (Van de Walle et al., Q-ForestLab; Zenodo 19630260, MIT licence) simulates air and surface temperature from a TLS voxel grid plus weather data. It has been built and run here as a test (Gontrode, 8 July 2023; model run 38 s). Its structural input is a 1 m voxel grid with point count and density per voxel — the same kind of grid the profile analysis above computes, so the four plots can be fed in directly.

  • Virtual disturbance: remove single canopy trees by their hand-labelled ID, re-run light and temperature, compare before/after.
  • Structure over time: the ForEdgeClim package ships TLS grids of Gontrode at seven dates (June 2023 – April 2025) and hourly microclimate measurements (TOMST) — open data to link changing structure with measured buffering.
  • Same weather, different structure: drive the model with one weather record and the four plot grids to isolate the structural effect.

Limitations

  • No scanner positions in the data → no ray tracing with occlusion correction; the upper canopy is underestimated, gaps rather overestimated. Values are relative.
  • Test areas are small (0.05–0.24 ha) and not representative of the full 1 ha plots.
  • Scan season is not documented here (leaf-on/leaf-off matters for Wytham Woods).
  • No microclimate measurements: light is a structural estimate, not a measured buffering.
  • Ofental: exact plot coordinates not available; latitude 47.6° used for the sun path.
