Benchmarks against ground truth

Two questions, datasets with real truth: Does the chain find the trees? (point clouds labelled per point or per tree: Vienna Woods, Robson Creek) and how accurately does it measure DBH? (single trees with an independent field inventory).

What the false alarms are

Because the dataset also labels the non-tree classes, we can say what each false alarm actually was instead of "probably deadwood" (baseline on plot_06, 56 false alarms):

ClassCount
Shrub / ground vegetation28
Low vegetation / lying deadwood10
Branches + leaves10
Non-vegetation, upright at breast height (pole/person)3
Thin tree / twig3

The mapping number → class name is derived, not documented: neither dataset nor paper contains a table. It comes from a geometric analysis (height above ground, number and radius of clusters in the breast-height band). That class 3 independently yields 68 and 128 stem cross-sections on the two plots — against ~69 and ~128 trees named in the paper — supports it.

Findings

  • 3DFin is clearly superior on plot-wide TLS clouds and stable across both plots: 67 of 68 and 121 of 128 stems, the latter without a single false alarm. The suite's own numpy baseline and the custom lidR workflow produce more false alarms than hits. In the tropical rainforest (Robson Creek) 3DFin loses 8–12 points of recall and 11–16 points of precision.
  • The stem continuity check works. The filter tests whether the stem continues above breast height: precision 42.3 → 70.9 %, 40 of 56 false alarms removed, at the cost of 2 real trees. The remaining false alarms are mostly branches — upright structures against which this criterion cannot help in principle.
  • To be fair to Csp: it is primarily a segmentation, not a stem detector. The low precision means it keeps many non-tree objects as separate segments. The supplied column quality_flag does not separate them (139 of 141 detections carry the same value).

Scope — and where it ends

These numbers apply to plot-wide TLS clouds with adequate stem circumference coverage. They do not transfer:

plot_06 beech360°86 %4,604
plot_07 spruce280°88 %3,263

By default 3DFin requires 9 of 16 sectors = 202° circumference coverage. At the Renon stand 28 % of the stems meet this; there 3DFin gives only 8 of 37 detections a diameter. A sweep over res_cloth = 0.45/0.70/1.00/1.50 changes nothing — the bottleneck is coverage, not configuration. The refusal is correct behaviour: the data do not support a diameter there. The flip side: our own methods still return numbers for exactly these stems, which then rest on 180° arcs.

Methods

  • Baseline Python — numpy circle fit on the breast-height slice, leaf-on whole cloud.
  • QSM wood Python — the same fit, but on wood points only (classification 0, SYSSIFOSS ground truth). By definition this is the QSM DBH (2 × median stem radius at 1.3 m).
  • 3DFin Python — 3DFin, based on the dendromatics library.
  • Csp R — CspStandSegmentation (Frey & Schindler, University of Freiburg), based on lidR.

Interpretation

  • All methods fail on the 11 cm sapling (QueRub_KA09): the stem is too thin and the crown reaches down to breast height. The leaf-on baseline fit gives a gross outlier (228 cm), the wood-only fit is better (91.8 cm) but still inaccurate; 3DFin and Csp return no result.
  • PinSyl_KA10 is overestimated by all methods (46–51 instead of 35 cm). The agreement between methods points to a property of the tree or a different field measurement height, not a method error.
  • QSM wood reaches the highest accuracy (5.7 cm) thanks to the leaf/wood separation and a clean stem. For comparison: the SYSSIFOSS paper reports 3.5 cm RMSE for its careful TLS method.

Agreement without field measurement (Vienna Woods)

For the correctly detected 3DFin stems of the two Vienna Woods plots there is no field DBH — only the agreement between methods can be measured there, not their accuracy. It is remarkably tight:

Values = mean absolute deviation from the consensus (median of the four methods) in cm. For comparison: at the Renon stand, on the stems of the suite's own detection, the median spread is 2.2 cm — detection quality carries through into the method comparison. Agreement is not accuracy: the methods can all be wrong together.

Limitations

  • n = 5: a field DBH is stored in the pytreedb database for only this many locally available trees. Small sample.
  • aRchi (R QSM library) has been archived from CRAN; the QSM DBH, however, corresponds to the wood-stem fit computed here.
  • 3DFin performs worse here than on the plot clouds (MAE 7.8 cm, worse than the simple circle fit). This is expected: SYSSIFOSS are isolated single trees, 3DFin is built for plot clouds. Misused, not refuted.
