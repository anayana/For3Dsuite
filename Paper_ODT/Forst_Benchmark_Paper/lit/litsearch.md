  - Abstract: <jats:p>This study evaluates whether handheld mobile laser scanning (HMLS) can support the design of canopy consolidation systems for urban Pinus pinea L. trees without climbing into the crown. Fifty stone pines in a historic park in Pistoia (Italy) were surveyed with a SLAM-based handheld laser scanner and assessed with Visual Tree Assessment; five trees were then selected for consolidation design. Tree height, diameter at breast height (DBH), and crown metrics were derived from the point cloud and compared with field measurements, and the scaffold branches were reconstructed as sequences of truncated cones. For each branch, the bending stress generated by self-weight was computed with cantilever-beam theory and compared with the compressive strength of green P. pinea wood; a wind load case and a sensitivity analysis to diameter errors were added. Under self-weight, the safety factor of the vulnerable branches ranged from 1.44 to 37; one branch was below the usual minimum of 1.5 and a second fell to 1.69 under the 27 m/s wind. Anchor positions, rope trajectories, and system classes (4 t or 8 t) were drawn directly on the point cloud. The study is proof of concept: the systems have not been installed, and the mechanical results have not been verified by load tests, but the workflow shows that the data needed for consolidation design can be obtained remotely and non-invasively.</jats:p>
- **A Comparative Analysis of UAV LiDAR and Mobile Laser Scanning for Tree Height and DBH Estimation in a Structurally Complex, Mixed-Species Natural Forest** — Lucian Mîzgaciu, Gheorghe Marian Tudoran, Andrei Eugen Ciocan, Petru Tudor Stăncioiu, Mihai Daniel Niță (2025). *Forests* 16(9) 1481 — https://doi.org/10.3390/f16091481
  - Abstract: <jats:p>Accurate measurement of tree height and diameter at breast height (DBH) is essential for forest inventory, biomass estimation, and habitat assessment but remains challenging in structurally complex, multi-layered forests. This study evaluates the accuracy and operational feasibility of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) LiDAR and Mobile Laser Scanning (MLS) for estimating tree height and DBH in such stands with a diverse structure in the Romanian Carpathians. Field measurements from six plots encompassing mixed-species (Fagus sylvatica L., Abies alba Mill., Picea abies (L.) H.Karst.) and single-species (Picea abies) stands were compared against UAV- and MLS-derived metrics. MLS delivered near-inventory-grade DBH accuracy across all species (R2 up to 0.98) and reliable height estimates for intermediate and suppressed trees, while UAV LiDAR consistently underestimated tree height, especially in dense, multi-layered stands (R2 &lt; 0.2 in mixed plots). Voxel-based occlusion analysis revealed that over 93% of area under canopy and interior crown volume was captured only by MLS, confirming its dominance below the canopy, whereas UAV LiDAR primarily delineated the outer canopy surface. Species traits influenced DBH accuracy locally, but structural complexity and canopy layering were the main drivers of height underestimation. We recommend hybrid UAV–MLS workflows combining UAV efficiency for canopy-scale mapping with MLS precision for stem and sub-canopy structure. Future research should explore multi-season acquisitions, improved SLAM robustness, and automated data fusion to enable scalable, multi-layer forest monitoring for carbon accounting, biodiversity assessment, and sustainable forest management decision making.</jats:p>
